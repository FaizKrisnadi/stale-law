Stale Law pilot — investment-protection due diligence for a Chinese investor in Indonesia

Revision notice, 10 November: This is a constructed research pilot. Some claims, especially jurisdiction, historical treaty status, administrative procedure and sector rules, remain provisional. The text and tables require independent legal review before public reliance.

Work product, 7 November 2026. Register closed against the instruments, 9 November 2026; T8 re-checked against the RCEP text, 12 November 2026. Prepared with AutoClaw (Legal Assistant). Constructed scenario, real law.

Status legend. VERIFIED = retrieved from a primary or official source this session, quote and URL attached. EXTERNAL = a named professional, academic or official source, not the instrument itself. UNVERIFIED = not retrieved this session; do not rely on it.

1. Mandate

A hypothetical Chinese manufacturing investor holds, through a Hong Kong holding company, a minority interest in an Indonesian PT PMA operating a nickel processing line in Central Sulawesi. During 2025–2026 the investment is hit by: a reduced ore quota (RKAB), a mandatory onshore retention of natural-resource export proceeds, a forestry-permit closure over part of the concession, and a proposed mandatory export intermediary. The investor asks four questions:

  1. What domestic statutory protection exists against expropriation or a destructive regulatory act?
  2. Where can a licence or quota decision be challenged, and in what window?
  3. Which treaty, if any, supports an investor-state claim as of November 2026?
  4. What has changed since March 2020 that a first-pass answer would miss?

The scenario is constructed for the pilot. No client, no real party, no confidential material. The law is real.

2. Method — the part other people can reuse

Six steps, applied in this order. Skip a step and the memo becomes a hallucination with footnotes.

  1. Fix the as-of date. "What is the law?" is not a question. "What is the law on 7 November 2026?" is.
  2. Ask the paired probe. Repeat the question as of an earlier date (here, March 2020). Compare the answers against the relevant law: some should differ and others may be the same.
  3. Retrieve the instrument, not the commentary. Ministry PDF, JDIH, treaty text. Commentary is for finding the instrument, not for replacing it.
  4. Quote, then gloss. Verbatim in the original language, then a short English gloss. Never paraphrase inside the quote.
  5. Mark what you did not get. An honest UNVERIFIED row is worth more than a confident reconstruction.
  6. Write the verification log. Record the answer a competent first pass would have given, and where the source broke it.

This pilot is the first worked example of the Stale Law research design (5 November 2026). It is not the study. The study needs a corpus, a ladder of interventions, and a second annotator.

3. Headline finding

A common risk in date-sensitive research is treating the 1994 China–Indonesia BIT as though its original term still applied. The baseline test, where conducted, is documented separately.

That treaty was terminated with effect from 31 March 2015. Investments made before termination were covered by a ten-year survival clause that itself expired on 31 March 2025. A measure Indonesia takes in 2026 cannot, as a general matter, be brought under the 1994 BIT.

One potential investor-state route is the ASEAN–China Investment Agreement, signed 15 August 2009 and in force by 2020; its precise entry-into-force date for Indonesia requires reconciliation. RCEP's investment chapter contains no investor-state dispute settlement; Article 10.18 commits the Parties only to discuss one.

Domestic law still says something. Article 7 of Law 25/2007 forbids nationalisation except by statute and promises compensation at market price, with arbitration if the parties cannot agree on the amount. That is a domestic statutory right, in a domestic or contractual forum. It is not a substitute for a treaty claim, and it does not survive a licence revocation in the same way.

Article 32 points the same way. It sends a foreign investor to international arbitration only where both sides agree to it — yang harus disepakati oleh para pihak — so the investment law provides a forum but not consent. Any consent must be established separately, for example in an applicable treaty or agreement. This makes the source and scope of any separate consent a question for the treaty or agreement and the facts.

The second error is the one worth the entry. Fixing the date is the easy half. A memo that then says "the ACFTA gives you FET, national treatment, MFN and protection against indirect expropriation" is quoting the summary, not the treaty. Read the articles and the protection narrows sharply: fair and equitable treatment is defined as an obligation not to deny justice in legal or administrative proceedings (Art. 7(2)(a)); national treatment is confined to management, conduct and operation, so admission and establishment are outside it (Art. 4); MFN carves out future agreements and intra-ASEAN preferences (Art. 5); Article 6 addresses existing and new non-conforming measures. Whether a quota cut gives rise to a claim under any provision depends on the facts, jurisdiction and legal interpretation.

In March 2020 the reported survival period was still running for qualifying investments. Article XIII(2) of the signed text continues Articles I–XII for investments made before termination. MOFCOM's English transcription reads "I to VI"; the Chinese text and Jus Mundi read I–XII, and the treaty says the English text prevails on divergence. The range includes Article IX, the dispute article: arbitration under Article IX(3) is limited to the amount of compensation for expropriation, and other disputes go to the host state's courts. Whether a particular investor and investment qualify requires separate investigation. (Corrected 13 November 2026: earlier versions of this memo said I–VI, following the MOFCOM English transcription alone. The discrepancy was surfaced by an AutoClaw research run and then checked against the three sources linked here.) The ASEAN–China agreement also applied by 2020.

4. Domestic law

4.1 Expropriation and compensation — VERIFIED

Law No. 25 of 2007 on Investment, Article 7, retrieved from JDIH Kementerian Keuangan:

Pasal 7

(1) Pemerintah tidak akan melakukan tindakan nasionalisasi atau pengambilalihan hak kepemilikan penanam modal, kecuali dengan undang-undang.

(2) Dalam hal Pemerintah melakukan tindakan nasionalisasi atau pengambilalihan hak kepemilikan sebagaimana dimaksud pada ayat (1), Pemerintah akan memberikan kompensasi yang jumlahnya ditetapkan berdasarkan harga pasar.

(3) Jika diantara kedua belah pihak tidak tercapai kesepakatan tentang kompensasi atau ganti rugi sebagaimana dimaksud pada ayat (2), penyelesaiannya dilakukan melalui arbitrase.

Gloss: the Government will not nationalise or take ownership rights except by statute; if it does, compensation is set at market price; if the parties cannot agree on compensation or damages, the dispute is settled by arbitration.

Limits to examine:

  • Article 7 is a direct taking rule. It does not, by its terms, define indirect or regulatory expropriation. Stretching it to cover a quota cut, an export-retention rule, or a forestry closure is interpretation, not text.
  • Article 7(3) arbitrates the amount of compensation, not the lawfulness of the taking. The lawfulness question sits with the administrative and constitutional courts.
  • The Cipta Kerja chain did not amend Article 7. UU 6/2023's investment cluster (Lampiran, Pasal 77) rewrites Articles 2, 12, 13, 18 and 25 of UU 25/2007 and then closes. The 2007 text quoted above is the text in force in November 2026. See §4.5.

4.2 National treatment — VERIFIED

Same source, Article 6:

(1) Pemerintah memberikan perlakuan yang sama kepada semua penanam modal yang berasal dari negara manapun yang melakukan kegiatan penanaman modal di Indonesia sesuai dengan ketentuan peraturan perundang-undangan.

(2) Perlakuan sebagaimana dimaksud pada ayat (1) tidak berlaku bagi penanam modal dari suatu negara yang memperoleh hak istimewa berdasarkan perjanjian dengan Indonesia.

Gloss: equal treatment for investors from any country, except where a country has special rights under an agreement with Indonesia. This is a most-favoured-nation style rule in the statute, and it is how a Chinese investor might reach a better treatment granted to a third country — but only through the statute, and only "according to laws and regulations," which is a wide gate for exceptions.

4.3 Sector access — VERIFIED, nickel rows included

Article 12 as enacted in 2007: all business fields are open except those closed or open-with-conditions, which the President sets by Presidential Regulation; the statute itself closed weapons, ammunition, explosives and war equipment to foreign investors, plus any field expressly closed by another statute. That wording is no longer current. UU 6/2023 rewrote Article 12 (§4.5): paragraph (1) now opens all fields except those declared closed to investment or reserved to the Central Government, and the article carries its own closed list, which this pilot did not quote. Do not cite the 2007 list as the law in 2026.

The operative list is Perpres 10/2021, amended by Perpres 49/2021. Both were retrieved, with all four lampiran files. Lampiran I (priority sectors) carries KBLI 24202, Industri Pembuatan Logam Dasar Bukan Besi, at No. 107 (hlm. 17). Its persyaratan column reads “Melakukan alih teknologi” — perform technology transfer. Its nickel sub-codes appear at hlm. 34–35: 24202B1, nickel metal from ore by pyrometallurgy (matte), and 24202B2, nickel metal from ore by hydrometallurgy (MHP). Neither carries a condition of its own.

No foreign-ownership percentage appears for KBLI 24202, 24202B1 or 24202B2 anywhere in Lampiran I, II or III. Lampiran II (MSME allocation) and Lampiran III (conditional list) do not contain the code at all. Perpres 49/2021 — the only amendment — changes Pasal 2, Pasal 6 and the alcohol rows (KBLI 11010, 11020, 11031). It does not touch 24202.

The working conclusion, and where it stops: equity in nickel matte or MHP is not capped by the positive list, and the sector's stated condition is technology transfer. That is what the instrument says. Whether a wholly foreign-owned PT PMA clears licensing under the risk-based framework is a separate question this pilot did not test.

4.4 Disputes with the Government — VERIFIED

Article 32 in full, from the JDIH Kementerian Keuangan text:

Pasal 32

(1) Dalam hal terjadi sengketa di bidang penanaman modal antara Pemerintah dengan penanam modal, para pihak terlebih dahulu menyelesaikan sengketa tersebut melalui musyawarah dan mufakat.

(2) Dalam hal penyelesaian sengketa sebagaimana dimaksud pada ayat (1) tidak tercapai, penyelesaian sengketa tersebut dapat dilakukan melalui arbitrase atau alternatif penyelesaian sengketa atau pengadilan sesuai dengan ketentuan peraturan perundang-undangan.

(3) Dalam hal terjadi sengketa di bidang penanaman modal antara Pemerintah dengan penanam modal dalam negeri, para pihak dapat menyelesaikan sengketa tersebut melalui arbitrase berdasarkan kesepakatan para pihak, dan jika penyelesaian sengketa melalui arbitrase tidak disepakati, penyelesaian sengketa tersebut akan dilakukan di pengadilan.

(4) Dalam hal terjadi sengketa di bidang penanaman modal antara Pemerintah dengan penanam modal asing, para pihak akan menyelesaikan sengketa tersebut melalui arbitrase internasional yang harus disepakati oleh para pihak.

Gloss: the parties deliberate first; failing that, arbitration, ADR or a court as the law provides; for a domestic investor, arbitration if agreed and otherwise the courts; for a foreign investor, international arbitration which the parties must agree to.

That last qualifier is the load-bearing four words in the article. Article 32(4) is not a standing consent to arbitrate. It tells a foreign investor where a dispute goes once both sides have agreed, and it supplies no consent of its own. A Chinese investor facing a quota cut in 2026 therefore cannot found an arbitration on the investment law alone. Consent must be established separately, for example in an applicable treaty or agreement. That is the whole reason §5 matters, and it is visible only if you read past Art. 32(1).

4.5 The Cipta Kerja chain — VERIFIED, and it stopped short of Article 7

The chain as history:

  • UU 11/2020 (Cipta Kerja) amended, among other things, the investment law's licensing architecture.
  • Constitutional Court Decision No. 91/PUU-XVIII/2020 declared the formation of UU 11/2020 conditionally unconstitutional and ordered a repair within two years.
  • Perppu 2/2022 filled the gap and became UU 6/2023.

The operative text in 2026 is UU 6/2023, and its investment cluster is short enough to read in one sitting. Lampiran, Pasal 77 amends UU 25/2007 in five numbered items, and then the cluster closes and moves to banking:

ItemArticle of UU 25/2007What changed
1Art. 2Scope: the law now applies to all sectors and is the primary reference
2Art. 12The closed list is restated; the President sets the list by Perpres
3Art. 13MSME partnership and empowerment duties
4Art. 18Investment facilities and the criteria for them
5Art. 25Company establishment and business licensing

Article 7 and Article 32 are not among them. The omnibus rewrote how an investor enters and gets licensed. It left the nationalisation guarantee and the dispute route exactly as enacted in 2007.

That is a finding, not a formality. It means the protection text a 2026 answer quotes is the right text, for a reason now on the record — and it means the drafting choice is visible: entry was liberalised and re-papered, protection was not touched. BPK's status record for UU 25/2007 lists UU 11/2020, Perppu 2/2022 and UU 6/2023 as the only amending instruments, with nothing in 2024–2026.

4.6 Administrative remedy — VERIFIED

Article 55 of UU 5/1986 states a 90-day period in the following terms. It is not a complete deadline calculation for every dispute:

Pasal 55 — Gugatan dapat diajukan hanya dalam tenggang waktu sembilan puluh hari terhitung sejak saat diterimanya atau diumumkannya Keputusan Badan atau Pejabat Tata Usaha Negara.

Gloss: a claim may be filed only within 90 days from the moment the decision of the administrative body or official is received or announced.

Article 55 has never been amended. UU 9/2004 rewrote 33 articles of the PTUN law (2, 4, 6, 7, 12–22, 26, 28–38, 42, 44–46, 53, 116); UU 51/2009 rewrote 18 (1, 9A, 14, 20, 21, 25, 28, 30, 32–36, 39B, 42, 43, 116, 135). Article 55 is in neither list, which is why the number has survived four decades and two amendments.

PERMA 6/2018 Article 5 addresses timing after administrative remedies and provides a different starting point for certain third parties. The applicable route, trigger and computation depend on the claimant and procedural facts; do not calculate a deadline from Article 55 alone.

4.7 The measures that actually bite in 2025–2026 — EXTERNAL

From King & Wood Mallesons, 2 September 2026 — a law-firm review, not the instruments:

  • The export-proceeds framework distinguishes sectors and exceptions. The coordinating ministry describes a 100% / 12-month baseline for non-oil-and-gas resources and a different 30% / 3-month baseline for oil and gas, with eligible exceptions. PP 21/2026 took effect on 1 June 2026. Application to the constructed investor has not been established.
  • PP 24/2026 concerns export arrangements. As enacted, Article 3 describes an export SOE as owner or sole intermediary, with price-setting and possible exceptions. A 6 November government update describes a transition through 2026, planned changes and commercial relations and payments remaining between exporter and buyer. The enacted text and the implementation update need to be read together. The pilot does not establish how this framework applies to a particular nickel product or transaction.
  • Divestment, quota and forestry examples require checking against the applicable instruments and facts. They are scenario prompts, not established findings about a real investment.

Each of these needs to be checked against peraturan.go.id before it goes into advice. The constructed scenario does not establish that a particular measure affected an actual investor.

5. Treaty layer

5.1 China–Indonesia BIT (1994) — TERMINATED, and the survival clause has run

Two independent sources agree:

  • UNCTAD treaty record: status terminated, unilaterally denounced, date of termination 31 March 2015.
  • King & Wood Mallesons, 2 September 2026: "The 1994 China-Indonesia Bilateral Investment Treaty was terminated with effect from 31 March 2015; investments made before that date benefited from a ten-year survival clause that itself expired on 31 March 2025. Measures taken by the Indonesian government after that date accordingly cannot, as a general matter, be brought under the 1994 BIT."

Indonesia's wider BIT-termination programme of the mid-2010s is documented in the academic literature; this session did not retrieve the termination instrument itself. The WTI record reproduces the treaty text and does not report termination. The 31 March 2025 date is stated by KWM and follows from the ten-year survival period in Article 13(2). Status: EXTERNAL, two concordant sources on the termination date. Retrieve the termination notification before citing the date in a filing.

5.2 ASEAN–China Investment Agreement — the live route, and a narrower one than the summaries

The agreement was in force by 2020, but its precise entry-into-force date for Indonesia requires reconciliation. ASEAN’s official register records 15 January 2010 and an Indonesian notification on 27 September 2010; Article 27 of the signed text sets out a different rule. Other records give 1 January 2010. Do not use a precise date without resolving this.

Treaty text retrieved from the WTI treaty record. Articles 3 to 8 are quoted below; each is a primary source.

Scope — Art. 3(2). The Agreement "shall apply to all investments made by investors of a Party in the territory of another Party, whether made before or after the entry into force of this Agreement," but "does not bind any Party in relation to any act or fact that took place or any situation that ceased to exist before the date of entry into force." Coverage of a particular investment and measure depends on the agreement’s entry into force for the relevant parties and the other jurisdictional requirements. A 2012 measure cannot be excluded merely because of the 2010 entry-into-force date. This is the provision that fills the hole the BIT's expiry left.

Fair and equitable treatment — Art. 7. "Each Party shall accord to investments of investors of another Party fair and equitable treatment and full protection and security." Then the definition: "fair and equitable treatment refers to the obligation of each Party not to deny justice in any legal or administrative proceedings." That is a denial-of-justice standard. How this wording applies to a particular measure or proceeding requires legal analysis.

Expropriation — Art. 8. No expropriation "or other similar measures" except for a public purpose, in accordance with domestic law, non-discriminatorily, and on payment of compensation amounting to "the fair market value of the expropriated investment at the time when expropriation was publicly announced or when expropriation occurred, whichever is earlier," freely transferable, with interest for delay. Note what the retrieved text does not say: the word "indirect" does not appear in Article 8 as retrieved. Secondary summaries routinely claim protection against indirect expropriation. Treat that as an argument to be built, not a text to be quoted.

National treatment — Art. 4. Confined to "management, conduct, operation, maintenance, use, sale, liquidation, or other forms of disposal." Admission and establishment are outside the clause. A complaint about being refused entry to a sector is not an Article 4 complaint.

MFN — Art. 5. Carve-outs at 5(2) (future agreements need not be extended), 5(3) (existing bilateral and regional preferences; intra-ASEAN preferences), and 5(4) (no obligation to extend dispute-resolution procedures).

Non-conforming measures — Art. 6. Articles 4 and 5 "shall not apply to" existing or new non-conforming measures, or their continuation or amendment. This is the widest door in the treaty, and it is why a foreign-ownership complaint needs the specific reservation, not the headline rule.

ISDS — Art. 14. Present. Article 14(1) addresses qualifying claims alleging breach of Articles 4, 5 or 7–10 causing loss or damage in relation to management, conduct, operation, sale or other disposition of an investment. Other conditions in Article 14 also apply (PRIMARY). Procedure from the KWM review (EXTERNAL): three-year limitation, mandatory consultation, six-month cooling-off, fork-in-the-road on domestic-court election, ICSID or UNCITRAL.

What this means. The correct 2026 answer is not "the ACFTA protects you." The ACFTA is one possible route. Its text and any claim require careful analysis of the investor, investment, facts, timing, jurisdiction and the applicable provisions. This pilot does not establish that it is the only route or decide the merits of a quota-cut claim.

5.3 RCEP — no investor-state route

The RCEP investment chapter does not contain ISDS. Chapter 10 runs from Article 10.1 to Article 10.18 and none of the eighteen articles provides for arbitration between an investor and a Party. Article 10.18 (Work Programme) commits the Parties to "enter into discussions on … the settlement of investment disputes between a Party and an investor of another Party" no later than two years after entry into force, and to conclude them within three years of starting. RCEP entered into force for Indonesia on 2 January 2023. Whether those discussions have produced anything was not checked in this pilot; 2020 and 2026 commentary places a possible mechanism around 2027. It is not a route for a 2026 claim. Status: VERIFIED (text); the timing of any future mechanism is EXTERNAL.

5.4 ICSID — Indonesia remains a Contracting State

Indonesia appears on the ICSID/8 list of Contracting States and Measures (July 2026). No denunciation by Indonesia was found this session. Status: EXTERNAL/official list; confirm on the ICSID member-states database before filing.

5.5 Other instruments — not analysed in depth

Treaty nationality and standing are unresolved. ASEAN–China Article 1(f) requires a juridical person to be organised under a Party’s laws and conduct substantive operations in its territory. A Hong Kong holding company is not automatically a qualifying mainland China investor merely because of its ownership; assess each potential claimant and the separate ASEAN–Hong Kong agreement. The 2021 Singapore–Indonesia BIT may also matter depending on the holding structure. Neither China nor Indonesia is a CPTPP party. This pilot does not analyse them. A first-pass answer that lumps them in as "plus RCEP and CPTPP" is noise.

7. Potential errors to test

See the verification log. The short version:

  1. It cites the 1994 BIT as if in force. E3 — superseded.
  2. It then describes the ACFTA in the words of the client alert: FET, NT, MFN, indirect expropriation. E4 — correct instrument, overstated rule. Art. 7(2)(a) is denial of justice; Art. 4 excludes admission; Art. 6 preserves non-conforming measures.
  3. It treats Article 7 as covering any regulatory mischief. E2 — valid provision, wrong question.
  4. It states an administrative-court deadline from Article 55 without considering PERMA 6/2018, administrative remedies, the claimant and the applicable trigger. E6 — right number, no instrument, no object.
  5. It offers RCEP as a claim route. E3 / E2.
  6. It answers March 2020 and November 2026 with the same paragraph. E5 — missed temporal status.

9. Lessons for the entry, and for anyone reusing this

  • Version of the law is not a detail. It is the answer.
  • Secondary sources are how you find the instrument. They are not the instrument.
  • A constructed scenario is fine if it is labelled. Invented doctrine is not.
  • The reusable output is the protocol plus the probe pack, not the prose.
